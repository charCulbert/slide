Tide · faces

Four ways to put the same engine on a face with three controls, a mix, and toggles. Each one draws the same picture from the same numbers. Drag on any picture: above the line for the left time, below for the right, the marker for repeats. Link ratio keeps R a multiple of L, link difference keeps the gap in milliseconds, off frees it with light snapping to nice ratios. The sliders and knobs are live too.

F1Three sliders under the picture

The current study, cut down. Time, Repeats and Wash as sliders with plain-word ends, Mix at the right, toggles in the header. Wash replaces four sliders. The right side is the lower bracket in the picture, dragged below the line, and one chip cycles link ratio, link difference, off. Ratio snaps lightly to 3:2, φ, 2:1 and friends.

F2The picture is the only control

No sliders. Time is the bracket, Repeats is the marker, Wash is a vertical drag anywhere in the field, which fattens the marks as you pull down. Numbers appear only while you drag. The right side is the lower bracket, with the same link chip. Everything else is a chip. This is the most radical and the most honest: if the picture really shows every parameter, it can also set every parameter.

F3Ableton device strip

The form factor Live and most hosts actually give a delay: one row, 170 px tall. The picture shrinks to a strip and three knobs sit to its right. The link mode is one cycling button. This tests whether the picture survives at small size, which decides whether it can be the identity of the plugin or only a feature of it.

F4Two pictures

Time and Repeats stay in the repeats picture. Wash gets its own small picture beside it: one repeat, drawn as the smear it would become, with tone as its brightness. Drag in either. This keeps every control visual without asking one picture to carry a third axis.

F5Big knobs under the picture

The picture on top, three large knobs under it, Mix smaller at the end. Both are live: drag the bracket and the Time knob turns, turn the knob and the bracket moves. The link chip sits between them so it reads as belonging to both. Knobs are what a hand expects on a delay, the picture is what the eye gets.

F6Faders beside the picture

Mixer logic. The picture takes the width, three tall faders stand to its right like a channel strip, Mix beside them. Faders give the biggest travel per control on a face this size, and vertical motion for Wash matches the drag-down gesture in the picture.

F7The sliders are the axis

Time and Repeats as full-width sliders that share the picture's time axis exactly, so the Time handle sits directly under the bracket and the Repeats handle directly under the marker. Move either and the other follows because they are the same number. Wash is the one slider with its own scale. This is the tightest coupling of control and picture without giving up a handle.

F8The whole UI is the picture

No panel, no rows. The plugin window is the field, edge to edge. Chips sit on it where they belong: the link chip under the right bracket, hold by the marker, sync and shape and medium along the top edge. Wash is a thin gauge on the right edge that you drag, Mix the same on the left. Every number appears next to the thing it describes and only while you touch it. This is F2 taken all the way.

My read: F8 is what the whole study has been pointing at, and it only works because the picture already carries every parameter. F7 is the new favourite among the ones with handles: the sliders and the picture are literally the same axis, so nothing has to be learned twice, and it still gives a real handle. F5 is the one that looks most like a plugin people already own. F6 earns its place only if the face is short and wide. F3 remains the test of whether the picture survives small.
